## [H3-metal Brings Native MiniMax-H3 Inference to Apple Silicon](https://github.com/antirez/h3.c) ⭐️ 8.0/10

H3-metal, a native inference engine for the MiniMax-H3 omni-modal model, has been released for Apple Silicon, enabling efficient video generation on Macs. The engine leverages Metal 4 TensorOps hardware and includes an int8 MLP engine with dynamic activation quantization. This development significantly lowers the barrier for running state-of-the-art video generation models locally on Apple hardware, which previously required high-end GPUs or cloud services. It empowers Mac users with high-memory configurations to perform AI video generation offline, potentially accelerating creative workflows and research. The engine dynamically quantizes activations, uses per-output-channel weight scales, and provides one scale per 1,024 channels for the sensitive FC2 input. Community reports indicate that generating a 9-second 480x864 clip at 20 steps takes over an hour on an M5 Pro 64GB MacBook Pro, and a 15-second 480p video takes about 1.5 hours on an M4 Max 128GB Mac Studio.

**Background**: MiniMax-H3 is an open-source omni-modal generative model that can understand and generate text, images, video, and audio, producing video with native stereo audio at up to 2K resolution and 15 seconds in length. Apple Silicon Macs use unified memory, allowing the GPU and CPU to access the same memory pool, which is beneficial for large models. H3-metal is built on Apple's Metal framework and MLX, which are designed for machine learning on Apple hardware.

## [AI API Flaw Exposes Encrypted Reasoning Traces and Leaked Passwords](https://the-decoder.com/but-marinade-and-leaked-passwords-are-what-researchers-found-in-chatgpts-hidden-reasoning/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Security researchers discovered a vulnerability in the APIs of OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google that allows extraction of encrypted reasoning traces and moving them between models. A scan of public sessions turned up dozens of passwords and API keys, and the traces reveal that the reasoning summaries users see often hide what the models are actually doing. This vulnerability affects major AI providers and exposes hidden model behavior, raising significant security and privacy concerns. It could enable model distillation, credential theft, and undermine trust in AI reasoning transparency. The attack works by injecting an encrypted reasoning trace from a given model into a weaker, less-guarded sibling model, forcing it to decode and output the trace verbatim in plaintext. This bypasses anti-distillation mechanisms and enables four distinct attack vectors, including circumventing safeguards and extracting proprietary reasoning.

rss · The Decoder — AI新闻 · Aug 11, 17:38

**Background**: Large language models (LLMs) from providers like OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google often use chain-of-thought reasoning to arrive at answers. To protect proprietary reasoning, these providers encrypt the reasoning traces and only show users a summary. This research reveals that these encrypted traces are interchangeable across sessions, users, and models, allowing attackers to replay them into weaker models to extract the hidden reasoning.
