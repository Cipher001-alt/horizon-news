## [Alpha 21264: NT's Greatest RISC Revisited](https://halfhill.com/byte/1998-12_alpha.html) ⭐️ 7.0/10

This retrospective article revisits the DEC Alpha 21264 CPU, launched in October 1998, highlighting its technical superiority and the market challenges it faced. It explores the 'what ifs' of its potential wider adoption, particularly in the Windows NT ecosystem. The Alpha 21264 represents a pivotal moment in CPU architecture history, showcasing how technical excellence alone does not guarantee market success. Understanding its story offers valuable lessons for today's hardware industry, especially regarding the balance between innovation and ecosystem compatibility. The Alpha 21264, codenamed EV6, was a 64-bit RISC microprocessor with out-of-order and speculative execution, featuring a seven-stage integer pipeline. Notably, AMD's Athlon (K7) used the same EV6 bus, and there were plans for Slot A Alphas that could work on Athlon motherboards with only a BIOS change.

hackernews · Lammy · Aug 9, 10:06 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49230022)

**Background**: The Alpha was a 64-bit RISC ISA developed by Digital Equipment Corporation (DEC) to replace their 32-bit VAX CISC computers. RISC architectures use a small set of simple, fixed-length instructions, enabling efficient pipelining and high clock speeds, which made Alpha one of the fastest processors of its time. However, it competed against the dominant x86 CISC architecture and the growing Wintel ecosystem, ultimately limiting its market penetration.
