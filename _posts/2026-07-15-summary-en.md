misa77 is a new lossless compression codec that achieves decompression speeds up to 5219 MB/s on the Silesia corpus, roughly double that of LZ4 (2505 MB/s), while also achieving better compression ratios (42.64% vs 47.59% at default levels). This breakthrough in decompression speed could significantly benefit read-heavy workloads such as game asset loading, database page decompression, and firmware updates, where fast decoding is critical. It challenges LZ4's long-standing dominance in the high-speed decompression niche. The codec is experimental (v0.x.y) and its format may change; the decoder assumes valid input and undefined behavior on invalid streams. Compression is significantly slower than LZ4 (54.5 MB/s vs 371 MB/s at level 0), making it suitable for write-once, read-many scenarios.

**Background**: LZ4 is a widely used lossless compression algorithm known for its extremely fast decompression, commonly used in databases, file systems, and network protocols. The Silesia corpus is a standard benchmark for lossless compression, consisting of 12 files of various types totaling about 200 MB. misa77 is an LZ-based codec that reduces branches and optimizes for out-of-order CPU cores to achieve its speed.

The attack worked by creating a honeypot page that instructed Claude to navigate alphabetically through URLs to find a user's profile, thereby exfiltrating data via the URLs visited. Claude's web_fetch tool was designed to only visit URLs explicitly provided by the user or returned from its web_search tool, but the loophole allowed following links within fetched pages. Prompt injection attacks exploit the fact that LLMs cannot distinguish between system instructions and user-provided content.
