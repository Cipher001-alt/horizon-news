## [IBM Claims World's First Sub-1nm Chip Technology](https://arstechnica.com/gadgets/2026/06/ibm-claims-worlds-first-sub-1-nanometer-chip-technology/) ⭐️ 9.0/10

IBM announced the world's first sub-1 nanometer chip technology, featuring a nanostack transistor architecture at the 0.7 nm (7 angstrom) node, which packs nearly 100 billion transistors on a chip. This breakthrough could dramatically improve chip performance and energy efficiency for AI data centers and other computing applications, continuing the trend of transistor scaling beyond traditional limits. The 0.7 nm node name does not correspond to a physical dimension; it indicates roughly double the transistor density of IBM's previous 2 nm chip announced in 2021. IBM has only demonstrated a process design kit (PDK) and test structures, not a production-ready fab.

rss · ArsTechnica — 深度科技 · Jun 25, 10:00

**Background**: Transistor node names (e.g., 7 nm, 5 nm) have long been decoupled from actual gate lengths, serving as marketing terms for density improvements. IBM's nanostack architecture builds transistors vertically in 3D, like skyscrapers, to achieve higher density without shrinking lateral dimensions. This approach is part of the industry's shift toward angstrom-era scaling, where dimensions approach atomic scales.

## [Zig's New bitCast Semantics and LLVM Backend Improvements](https://ziglang.org/devlog/2026/#2026-06-25) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Zig introduced endian-agnostic bitCast semantics and LLVM backend improvements on June 25, 2026, simplifying bit-level data manipulation and enhancing incremental compilation. This change makes Zig more portable and predictable for systems programming, especially for handling binary protocols and packed data structures across different architectures. The new bitCast semantics treat bit representations logically, ignoring target endianness, so operations like bitcasting [2]u8 to u16 behave identically on all targets. LLVM backend improvements include better incremental compilation support.

hackernews · kouosi · Jun 25, 14:19 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48673825)

**Background**: bitCast is a built-in function in Zig that reinterprets the bits of a value as a different type. Previously, its behavior depended on the target's endianness, leading to non-portable code. Zig also supports arbitrary-width integers (e.g., u4, i13) which are lowered to LLVM IR's bit-int types.
