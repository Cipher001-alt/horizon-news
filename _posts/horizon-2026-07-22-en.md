## [MUD Game Used to Evaluate LLMs for $99](https://cruciblebench.ai/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Researchers used a 1970s text-based MUD game to evaluate large language models, spending only $99 on API credits. The experiment revealed that LLM-based classifiers used for evaluation can be unreliable, with inter-rater agreement as low as 22% between two judges. This low-cost proof of concept highlights a critical flaw in many AI benchmarks that rely on LLM judges: the classifiers themselves may introduce bias or noise, potentially distorting model rankings. It also demonstrates that classic text games can serve as novel, interpretable evaluation environments. The study measured LLMs on four behavioral dimensions, two of which used an LLM classifier. Removing those two dimensions caused a frontier model to drop six positions. The aggregate Cohen's kappa for probe detection was 0.04, indicating near-random agreement, and the model most affected shared a model family with the classifier.

**Background**: MUD (Multi-User Dungeon) is a text-based multiplayer game originating in 1978, where players explore and interact using typed commands. LLM classifiers are AI models used to evaluate other AI outputs, but they can suffer from bias and unreliability. Cohen's kappa is a statistical measure of inter-rater agreement that accounts for chance.
